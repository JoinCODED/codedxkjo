Blocks 2 and 3: What a dashboard is for

A dashboard is not a report of everything in the file.
It is the smallest set of charts that answers one decision.
Reports answer what happened. Dashboards answer what do I do.
Every chart answers exactly one question. If a chart answers no question, delete it. If it answers three, split it.

The three failures

FailureWhat it looks like
The chart with no questionIt is there because the field existed
The title that names a field"Count of RequestID by Branch" describes the axes. It says nothing
The unsorted comparisonAlphabetical bars, so the worst performer sits wherever the alphabet put it

Block 5: Which chart, and when

The decision rule

Your claim is aboutDraw
Comparing amounts across groupsBar chart, sorted by size
Change over timeLine chart
A relationship between two numbersScatter plot
Part of a whole, four groups or fewerBar chart, still. Not a pie
One number that matters on its ownNo chart. Say the number
The chart follows the claim. You do not pick a chart and then find something to put in it.

The title is where the finding goes

TitleWhat it does
"Count of RequestID by Branch"Names the axes. The reader does the work
"Requests by Branch"Shorter. Same problem
"Central Branch carries a quarter of all requests"States the finding. The reader is done
If your title could sit on top of any chart in the file without changing, it is not a title. It is a label.

Three smaller things

Start bar chart axes at zero. A truncated axis makes a two percent difference look like a crisis.
Put the row count on the dashboard. You have been writing it down for two days.
Color is for one thing at a time. If everything is highlighted, nothing is.
Where does the rule break?

Blocks 8 and 9: The capstone

The brief

Your reader: a branch operations manager. Has eleven minutes. Was not in this room.
The decision: where to put the next engineer.
Deliver: no more than four charts, and one sentence at the top saying what it means.
Every chart needs: a title that states a finding, a sensible sort, and a row count on the page.
Beside it: your change log. What you cleaned and why.
Clean it first. You will want to skip that and go to the charts.

One line held all three days

The prompt is the product.
Monday: same file, two prompts, two different dashboards.
Tuesday: three lines added to a question, and a hidden trap became visible.
Today: same charts, different titles, and one of them a stranger could read.
The work was never in the tool. It was in what you asked it for, and how precisely you asked. That does not change when the models change, and they will change.

The honest limit

Three days does not make you a data analyst.
It makes you someone who can no longer be handed a number and be quiet about it. That is worth more than it sounds.
